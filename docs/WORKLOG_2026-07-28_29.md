# Work report — 28–30 July 2026

Two working days, covering the label pipeline, the benchmark, and a proposed
method. Written to be checkable: every claim here has a file, a number, or a
command behind it.

**Headline:** the labelling policy was rebuilt to the maintainer's
specification; the benchmark implementations were audited against their source
papers and **eight defects were found and fixed**, two of which meant a method
had never run at all; a benchmark-wide evaluation flaw was found that had been
suppressing two methods by up to 0.16 PC; and one novel method was proposed
with its ablations.

---

## 1. Labelling policy (day 1)

Implemented the policy as specified: keep raters with few ratings, drop raters
using fewer than 3 distinct scores, require 10 ratings per image, and
normalise each rater's scale before averaging.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Rater dropped for low volume | <10 ratings | **no volume rule** |
| Rater dropped for no variation | ≤2 distinct **and** ≥10 ratings | **<3 distinct, any volume** |
| Image needs a label | 1 rating | **10 ratings** |
| Averaging | plain mean | **per-rater normalisation** |

**Result:** 64,624 ratings from 522 raters over 2,018 images. 106 raters
returned, 64 were removed, 449 images lost their label. Splits went
1,719/218/523 → **1,399/185/434**.

**The tension that had to be solved.** Keeping low-volume raters and
z-scoring them are directly incompatible — the returning raters have a median
of 3 ratings and one has a single rating, which has no standard deviation to
divide by. `legacy/normalization.py` shrinks each rater's statistics toward
the global ones in proportion to their evidence, so a heavy rater keeps their
own and a light one is barely adjusted rather than wildly adjusted.

**Evidence it is sound:**
- An independent method (joint affine ALS, `score_adjusted`) agrees at
  **r = 0.976**.
- `score` equals the expectation of its soft label to **1.8×10⁻¹⁵**, verified
  on every protocol load.
- The shrinkage constant is chosen by 5-fold held-out prediction, not by
  argument (below).

**Still open:** the assignment-confounding diagnostic is r = 0.120,
p = 0.033 — part of a rater's mean reflects *which images they saw*, so
`score` is slightly over-corrected. Unresolved and documented.

## 2. Defects found and fixed

### In the label pipeline

**Distribution-learning methods were graded on a target they were never
given.** `score` is a mean of *normalised* ratings, but the shipped
distribution counted *raw* ratings, so its expectation was `score_raw_mean`.
An LDL method trains on the distribution and was then scored against `score`.
Measured cost: **0.09 MAE (~16%)** for correctly hitting the target it was
handed.

Fixed at the root — clipping now happens per rating rather than after
averaging, so the label is exactly the mean of a mean-preserving soft-binned
distribution. The protocol now **verifies expectation == label on every load**,
and that check immediately caught a second bug: two builders were normalising
over different rating sets (0.09 drift).

**The shrinkage constant was never validated.** `k = 10` was chosen by analogy
to the image threshold. Held-out rating prediction says **k = 5**, now the
default. The honest finding, though, is that **it barely matters**: k = 0 to
k = 20 all sit within 0.1% of the best. It is not a tuned knob. What the
measurement does rule out is heavy shrinkage (k ≥ 50).

**Stale shipped summaries.** `COVERAGE.json` claimed 2,492 images and
pre-consolidation splits; `cropped_256` metadata had 2,469 rows for 2,467
crops. Both go stale structurally because they are written mid-pipeline.
`scripts/data/refresh_coverage.py` now derives them — and the dataset card —
from what is actually on disk, and errors if an image has no metadata row. It
also declares `cropped_256` as a Hugging Face config, which was missing
entirely; all three configs verified to load.

### In the benchmark implementations

Audited all 15 against their source publications **before** producing any
result. Five papers verified verbatim (ComboLoss, UOL, FPEM, SCUT-FBP5500,
AaNet); three not retrievable (R3CNN paywalled, TransFBP too recent, FPEM's
base learning rate unstated).

1. **`fpem` and `transfbp` had never been executed.** Both crashed on first
   real use — a device mismatch and an unknown backbone — despite being
   written, reviewed, and documented as ready.
2. **Extra submodules were collected by a hardcoded name list.** Anything not
   called `embedding` or `gate` was never moved to the device, never given to
   the optimiser, and never restored with the best epoch. `fpem`'s entire
   fusion stack would have trained at random initialisation; `aanet` was
   silently restoring a *mixture* of two epochs.
3. **`comboloss` optimised a different objective from its paper.** One head
   instead of two — which makes `L_reg` and `L_exp` mathematically identical
   and deletes the term tying the two outputs together — plus missing α/β/γ
   coefficients, MSE where the paper uses L1, and unweighted cross-entropy.
4. **`uol`'s setup file asserted something false.** It recorded "no published
   hyperparameters to quote"; the paper states all of them, and specifies
   **VGG16**, not the ResNet-18 that was running.
5. **Evaluation preprocessing did not match training.** Four methods trained
   on resize-256-then-crop and were evaluated on a plain resize — a
   self-inflicted domain shift.
6. **`gan2014`'s autoencoder target was attached to the graph**, letting
   stage 1 minimise its loss by collapsing the backbone toward a constant.
7. **The reproducibility flag was self-triggering.** `git_tree_dirty` scanned
   the whole tree, so a run writing its own results into `reports/` marked
   every subsequent result irreproducible because of its own output.
8. **Early stopping was crippling the SGD methods** — see §4, the largest
   single finding.

Full write-up: `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_VS_PAPERS.md`.

## 3. Benchmark results

All 15 methods executed on the corrected labels
(`docs/RESULTS.md`, `reports/benchmark/`). Top of the table:

| Method | PC | MAE | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `uol` | 0.798 | 0.583 | +0.042 from the backbone fix |
| `fpem` | 0.774 | 0.617 | architecture only — no pretrained priors |
| `ldl-ren2017` | 0.773 | 0.611 | MAE improved 0.651 → 0.611 from the target fix |
| `transfbp` | 0.749 | 0.646 | TransMix omitted |
| `comboloss` | 0.730 | 0.704 | |

Two audit fixes are visible directly in the numbers: `uol` gained **+0.042 PC**
from correcting its backbone to the paper's VGG16, and `ldl-ren2017`'s MAE

~0.09 predicted.

Non-deep methods also ran: `kagian2008` 0.444, `eisenthal2006` 0.432,
`fan2012` 0.410, against a `mean-baseline` floor of 0.000. Landmark geometry
alone carries roughly 55–60% of a fine-tuned CNN's correlation.

## 4. The evaluation flaw (largest finding)

`cnn-resnext50` scored **0.746 in one sweep and 0.602 in the next**, same seed,
same code, with only a tiny label change between them. A 0.145 swing is
variance, not capability.

Diagnosis: patience-5 early stopping on a **185-image** validation split halts
inside the noisy opening of a high-learning-rate SGD run. Confirmed by
re-running the three SGD methods with `--patience 15 --min-epochs 20`:

| Method | patience 5 | patience 15 | Δ | epochs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cnn-resnet18` | 0.5236 | **0.6873** | **+0.164** | 11 → 40 |
| `cnn-resnext50` | 0.6016 | **0.7100** | **+0.108** | 9 → 26 |
| `aanet` | 0.4885 | 0.4660 | −0.023 | 11 → 40 |

`aanet` getting slightly *worse* is the control that rules out regression to
the mean: the two methods that were halting at 9–11 epochs gained enormously,
while the one that is genuinely weak stayed weak.

**Consequence:** the published-schedule numbers for those methods were
measuring where early stopping happened to fire. The full table is being
re-run under the corrected rule, applied uniformly.

## 5. Proposed method: RW-LDL

**The gap.** MEBeauty records how many raters produced each label — 10 to 102,
a **4.3× spread in standard error**. Every method in the table, including
published LDL, discards this and treats all labels as equally reliable.

**Two changes, one idea — take the sampling process seriously:**

1. *Fit the counts, not the histogram.* If the model predicts `p` and raters
   are a sample from it, the counts follow Multinomial(n, p) with NLL
   `−Σ c_k log p_k` — the same expression as cross-entropy against the
   histogram but **weighted by n**. Reliability weighting is not an add-on; it
   falls out of writing the correct likelihood. KL to a normalised histogram
   is the approximation.
2. *Weight regression by label precision.* `w = 1/(se² + s0²)` with `s0` the
   median standard error, floored so one tightly-agreed image cannot dominate.

`rw-ldl` is identical to `ldl-ren2017` in backbone, head, optimiser and
reported statistic, so the comparison isolates the loss. Two ablations
(`rw-ldl-noweight`, `rw-ldl-kl`) determine which half does the work.

**Honest scope:** multinomial likelihood and inverse-variance weighting are
both standard statistics; neither is invented here. The contribution is the
observation that this literature discards reliability information it already
ships, plus a clean measurement of whether exploiting it helps. Results
pending.

**Also added, and explicitly *not* novel:** `dinov2-linear` / `dinov2-partial`
(frozen foundation features) and a rank-averaging ensemble. Labelled "NOT a
published FBP method" wherever they appear. Motivated by MD-Net's own ablation,
where the pretrained prior contributes more than the architecture.

## 7. What is NOT done

**No implementation has been checked against a published number.**
`validate_on_scut.py` exists for exactly this and needs SCUT-FBP5500, which
requires accepting a release agreement. Until it runs, a low score cannot be
distinguished from a broken implementation — not hypothetical, given
`comboloss`.

**Twelve of fifteen published methods have no test of their objective.** Only
`comboloss`, `ldl-ren2017` and `r3cnn` have their loss asserted against a
formula.

**Two results are undiagnosed.** `fpem` topped the table after I predicted it
would underperform badly; `aanet` scores below 2008 geometry.

**No significance analysis yet.** With n = 434 the leading entries sit inside
each other's intervals. `compare.py` is written and must run before any method
is described as beating another.

**Release blockers untouched:** the Unsplash ML-training clause (54% of
images), consent over depicted persons, and 1,795 images never screened for
public figures. See `docs/DATASET_PROBLEMS_2026-07-28.md`.

**Nothing is pushed.** One commit (`1d02a24`) on `chore/legacy-inventory`;
19 files uncommitted pending the current sweep.
